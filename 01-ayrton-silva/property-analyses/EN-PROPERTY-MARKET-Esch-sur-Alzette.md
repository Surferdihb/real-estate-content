# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**The one-line verdict.** Esch is the most affordable entry point in Luxembourg —
about €700/m² below the national average — and that is the main reason to buy
here. The honest counterweight is a local unemployment rate structurally above
the national average.

**The five numbers that matter.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Average asking price | **€7,149/m²**, −1.31% year on year |
| Average notarial price | **~€6,200/m²** — a €950/m² gap with asking |
| Correction since Jan 2026 peak | **−5.4%** (peak was €7,556/m²) |
| Gain since 2019 | **+47.1%** — the decade's gain is largely intact |
| Average asking rent | **€28.17/m²/month**, +3.22% year on year |

**Prices down, rents up.** That is the right direction for an investor, and it
still isn't enough in most of the commune.

**Esch is not one market, it's ten.** A 1.75× spread separates the top and
bottom neighbourhoods, and a 2.0× spread separates their rents. Al-Esch — the
historic centre — is the only zone where prices are still rising (+1.1%) *and*
where rent is double everywhere else (€27.70/m² against €14–18). It is the only
part of Esch where gross yield approaches 5%. Everywhere else runs 2.3–3.9%,
where bank leverage absorbs the entire operating result.

Belval-Université disappoints, and that matters. The district carrying the whole
southern development story sells *below* the commune average at €4,911/m² and
rents poorly at €15.75/m². Prices flat at −0.0%. New supply has caught up with
demand.

**The strategy that works here** is primary residence, or buy-to-let in Al-Esch
only. Not elsewhere in the commune.

**Supply and demand are opposite by side of the market.** 792 properties for
sale against 45 to rent — a 17.6:1 ratio. Buyers have real negotiating room.
Renters have almost none.

**The dominant risk is regulatory, not market.** Draft law 8184 would cut the
rent cap from 5% to 3.5% of revalued invested capital. On the reference
property that moves legally collectible rent from €2,869 to €2,126 per month —
**€8,916 less per year**, on any new lease. The bill is under discussion in the
Chamber of Deputies, **not passed**, and not retroactive. No buy-to-let decision
should be made before its fate is known.

**Second-order risk: the Belval catalyst is spent.** Less than 15% of the
masterplan remains. Buying Esch today on the thesis that "Belval will lift
everything" is arriving fifteen years into the story.

**12-month outlook.** Prices −2% to +1%. Rents +2% to +5%, unless 8184 passes.

**What would invalidate this thesis:** passage of law 8184, a shock to Esch
employment, or an ECB reversal.

**Two figures in this document are not current** and are flagged in place: the
local unemployment rate (8.9%, dated May 2024) and the commune-level building
permit data, which could not be extracted. Confirm both at source before using
this with a client.

## Translator's notes

Luxembourg-specific terms kept in French throughout, because they are legal
categories with no English equivalent:

- **Bëllegen Akt** — a registration-duty tax credit for owner-occupiers, worth
  up to €40,000 per buyer. Does not apply to buy-to-let.
- **Droits d'enregistrement** — registration duty, 7% in Luxembourg. Paid by
  the buyer on top of the price.
- **Prix notarié** — the price actually recorded at deed. Published aggregated
  and quarterly by the Observatoire de l'Habitat, with a lag. This is not a
  transaction register: individual sale prices are never public.
- **CPE** (*certificat de performance énergétique*) — energy performance
  certificate, graded A to I. Roughly equivalent to an EPC.
- **VEFA** (*vente en l'état futur d'achèvement*) — off-plan sale.
- **Copropriété / syndic** — the co-ownership structure of an apartment
  building and its managing agent.
- **PAG / PAP** — communal and local land-use plans, consultable on
  geoportail.lu.

# FULL TRANSLATION

## Methodological warning — what Luxembourg does not publish

The Anglo-Saxon market analysis model rests on four metrics that **do not exist
here**, and that it would be dishonest to fabricate:

| Metric | Status in Luxembourg |
|---|---|
| Sale-to-list price ratio | **Impossible** — no public register of transaction prices, no MLS |
| % of properties sold above asking | **Impossible** — same reason |
| Average days on market | **Not published** — not by the Observatoire, STATEC, or the Chambre Immobilière |
| Months of inventory / absorption rate | **Not published by commune** — transactions are only released at national and cantonal level |
| % cash buyers | **Not published** |

The figures below therefore rest on two sources of different natures: **asking
prices** (immotop.lu, athome.lu — what sellers want) and **aggregated notarial
prices** from the Observatoire de l'Habitat (what actually gets paid, published
quarterly with a lag). The gap between the two is itself information: in Esch
it is roughly €950/m².

The canton of Esch remains **the country's most accessible entry point, roughly

## 2. Supply and demand

**792 properties for sale against 45 to rent.** This 17.6:1 ratio is less
extreme than Niederkorn's (30:1) but tells the same story, structural to
Luxembourg: the **statutory rent cap** — 5% of revalued invested capital, law of
21/09/2006 — compresses yield to the point where owners prefer to sell rather
than let.

The consequences are opposite depending on which side of the market you are on:

- **Buying: a buyer's market.** Large stock, prices down over 1 and 3 years, a
  5.4% correction since the January peak. Negotiating room is real.
- **Renting: a landlord's market.** 45 properties for a commune of nearly
  38,000 inhabitants, rents at an all-time high (+30.8% over 5 years).

**Not calculable:** months of inventory and absorption rate. No transaction
series by commune is published. The 792 listings cannot be converted into a
clearance period without monthly sales data, and estimating it would be
invention.

## 3. New construction

**Belval — the catalyst is reaching maturity.** The Fonds Belval has overseen
**€3.2 billion of cumulative investment across 120 hectares** of former
steelworks brownfield, and **less than 15% of the original masterplan remains to
be developed**. €180 million is committed to infrastructure for 2025–2026. The
stated target remains 25,000 jobs and 7,000 residents.

This reads two ways, and the second matters more to a 2026 buyer: **the South's
principal value driver has already delivered most of its effect.** The flat
−0.0% price and weak rent at Belval-Université confirm it. Buying Esch today on
the bet that "Belval will lift everything" is arriving fifteen years after the
story began.

**Building permits:** STATEC publishes permits by canton (last updated July
2026, data.public.lu). Commune-level detail for Esch could not be extracted in
this research — **pull it directly from data.public.lu** before making an
argument of it. Nationally, the VEFA decline is clear: **207 transactions in Q1
2026, −18.2% year on year**, against +9.4% for existing apartments. New supply
is under pressure while resale picks up.

Since 2026, the principle of **"silence means consent"** applies to building
permits, with digitisation via MyGuichet and regulated processing times —
favourable to renovation and conversion projects.

The average commune rent is at an **all-time high in July 2026**. The recent
trough dates to September 2024 (€24.55/m²): +14.7% in under two years.

**A caveat on the commune average.** The €28.17/m² figure is an average pulled
upward by Al-Esch; eight zones out of ten rent between €14 and €18/m². A
secondary source in fact places Esch rent at around **€20/m²** (Observatoire,

average of the listings. **For any modelling, use the figure for the property's
zone, never the commune average.**

### The statutory cap applied to the reference property

```
Invested capital = €675,000 + €47,250 (7% duty) + €6,750 (notary ~1%)
                 = €729,000
STATEC coefficient (2026 purchase) ≈ 1.00
```

| Regime | Annual rent cap | Monthly rent cap |
|---|---|---|
| **5% — in force** | €36,450 | **€3,037** |
| **3.5% — draft law 8184** | €25,515 | **€2,126** |

Market rent at Al-Esch zone rates: €27.70 × 103.59 m² = **€2,869/month**.

- Under the current regime, the market **falls just below the cap** (€2,869
  against €3,037): the market sets the rent, with a margin of only 6%.
- Under the reformed regime, the market **would exceed the cap by 35%**. Legally
  collectible rent would drop to €2,126 — **€743 less per month, €8,916 per
  year**.

**Gross yield at market rent: 5.10%.** That is the top of the Luxembourg range
(2–3.5% in Luxembourg City) and it rests entirely on the property being in the
Al-Esch zone. The same property in Belval-Université would yield 3.85%.

**Short-term letting:** check the Esch communal regulation before any modelling.
Several Luxembourg communes regulate or restrict short-term letting, and that
verification has not been done here.

## 8. Risk factors

1. **Regulatory risk — draft law no. 8184. SEVERITY: HIGH.**
   Moving the cap from 5% to 3.5% for new leases would cut collectible rent on
   the reference property by €743/month. The text is under discussion in the
   Chamber of Deputies, **not passed**, not retroactive. It is the most
   quantifiable risk in the file.

2. **Economic concentration and local unemployment. SEVERITY: HIGH.**
   Communal unemployment structurally above the national average, employment
   base dominated by Belval and the public sector. The available figure (8.9%)
   is stale and must be refreshed.

3. **Exhaustion of the Belval catalyst. SEVERITY: MEDIUM.**
   Less than 15% of the masterplan remains. Most of the revaluation
   attributable to the project is already in prices — and Belval-Université, at
   −0.0% and €15.75/m² rent, suggests new-build there has saturated demand.

4. **Negative price trend. SEVERITY: MEDIUM.**
   −1.3% over 1 year, −6.3% over 3 years, −4.0% over 5 years, and −5.4% since
   the January 2026 peak. Buying today is not buying a confirmed bottom.

5. **Intra-communal dispersion. SEVERITY: MEDIUM.**
   1.75× on prices and 2.0× on rents between zones. Any analysis based on the
   commune average is wrong for a given property. The risk is paying Al-Esch
   prices for a property in Brill.

6. **Rate sensitivity. SEVERITY: MEDIUM.**
   Average BCL rate on new lending: **3.01% in January 2026**, down 14 basis
   points on the month. The recovery in volumes is recent and fragile; an ECB
   reversal would stop it.

7. **Provisional CPE. SEVERITY: MEDIUM (property-specific).**
   The stated B/C is provisional and the renovation is ongoing. A downgraded
   definitive certificate would change the price positioning.

8. **Flood zones. SEVERITY: TO BE DETERMINED.**
   The Alzette runs through Esch. The parcel's classification has not been
   verified — **consult geoportail.lu** (PAG/PAP layers and flood zones) before
   any transaction.

9. **Mobilisation tax** on vacant housing and undeveloped land, legislated in
   stages. **SEVERITY: LOW to MEDIUM**, application status to be reconfirmed.

10. **Property tax. SEVERITY: LOW.**
    Very low in Luxembourg, often a few hundred euros a year. Never carry over
    the American assumption of 1–2% of value annually.

## 9. 12-month outlook

*Projections, not certainties.*

**Prices.** Stabilising to slightly negative, in a **−2% to +1%** range.
Arguments for decline: 792 properties in stock, a 3-year trend of −6.3%, a
correction underway since January. Arguments for increase: rates at 3.01% and
falling, national resale volumes recovering (+9.4% on existing apartments),
structural housing shortage.

**Supply.** Elevated stock maintained. VEFA down 18.2% will reduce new-build
supply at 12–24 months, supporting existing stock in the medium term.

**Rental demand.** Continued tension. With 45 properties available and a
statutory cap that discourages letting, rental scarcity will not resolve itself.
Rent growth expected between **+2% and +5%**, unless law 8184 is adopted.

**Rate scenarios.** At 3.0%, solvent demand holds. A return towards 3.5% would
restore downward pressure on prices; a fall below 2.75% would probably trigger
the rebound that 2025 had sketched out.

**What would invalidate this thesis:** adoption of law 8184 (collapse of
investor demand), a shock to Esch employment, or an ECB reversal.

## 10. In summary

Esch-sur-Alzette is a **buyer's market for sales and a landlord's market for
rentals**: 792 properties in stock with prices down 6.3% over three years on one
side, 45 properties to rent with rents at an all-time high on the other. The
entry point is the country's most accessible — roughly €700/m² below the
national average — and that is the main reason to buy here; communal
unemployment structurally above the national average is the honest counterpart.

**The strategy that works here is purchase as a primary residence, or buy-to-let
in the Al-Esch zone only**, the sole neighbourhood where gross yield reaches 5%.
Elsewhere in the commune, between 2.3% and 3.9%, bank leverage absorbs the
entire operating result. The reference property at €6,516/m² is correctly
positioned: below the average of communal listings, with a B/C energy rating
that clearly distinguishes it from 1950s stock.

**The principal risk is neither price nor location: it is draft law no. 8184.**
It would bring legally collectible rent on this property from €2,869 to €2,126
per month — €8,916 less annual income — on any new lease. No buy-to-let
decision should be taken before its fate is known.
